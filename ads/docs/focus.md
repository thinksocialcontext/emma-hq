# F.O.C.U.S. angles for Earrings by Emma

Every ad answers one reason to buy. Tag each ad with one letter and keep the batch balanced across letters, or deliberately test one.

| Letter | Means | Earrings by Emma example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F** Frustration | the pain they live with now | Every pair makes her ears itchy and red by lunch. |
| **O** Outcome | the life after | "For the first time in 30 years, I can wear pierced earrings again." |
| **C** Constraint | what stops them buying, removed | Worried it won't work? The store refunds up to 2 worn pairs that bothered you. |
| **U** Unspoken cost | the hidden cost of doing nothing | Holes closing up. Gold pairs at fifty dollars and up. Skipping earrings at the wedding. |
| **S** Skepticism | "I've tried everything" | Titanium, surgical steel, clear nail polish. What worked: no metal touching the ear. |

## Buyer groups to rotate (see memory `customers-and-voice`)

* gave up on earrings for years
* tried every metal
* parent of a kid with sensitive ears
* collector, teacher, themed wearer (holidays, school, Classical Conversations, sports)
* gift buyer

## Hooks

Adapt a proven hook pattern to an angle; do not invent from nothing. Good patterns for this brand:

* the confession: "I haven't worn earrings in 20 years."
* the list of failures: "Titanium. Surgical steel. Nail polish. Then this."
* the kid quote: "Mom, they don't itch!" (write it as a sample line, never present it as a real quote)
* the review card: a real review, first name and last initial, from the site
* the objection answer: "Plastic? They don't look cheap."
* the math: Buy 3, get 1 free (only while the offer is live)

Keep a running file of hooks that won in `research/hooks-that-won.md`, with the ad, dates and numbers.
